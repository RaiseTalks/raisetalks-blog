## Fundraising runs on connected software now

Not long ago, a data room was a folder an analyst read on a Friday afternoon. Today the first reader is often software: an associate's AI assistant summarising forty rooms, a screening tool scoring them, a CRM logging which ones move forward.

That changes what a good data room is. It has to be **structured**, so software can read it. It has to be **connected**, so revenue and usage come from the system that produced them, not from a spreadsheet typed the night before. And it has to be **open**, so your own tools and your investors' tools can work with it. More on that shift in [what AI agents change about diligence](/blog/what-ai-agents-change-about-diligence).

The best software in every category already works this way: open standards, public APIs, data pulled from the source of truth, and no lock-in. RaiseTalks is built to the same standard, for fundraising.

1. **Structured for investors.** 143 fields in the order investors read them: company, team, market, product, traction, financials, deal terms and cap table. Upload your deck and AI fills the room in about a minute, then lists what it could not find.

5. **Connected, and getting more connected.** Your AI assistant already works with the room through MCP. Revenue, analytics and the VC tools investors diligence with are next, from more than 400 integrations we have mapped. This is the part that compounds.
