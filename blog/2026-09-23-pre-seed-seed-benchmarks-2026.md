The median seed round in 2026 is roughly $4.1 million at a $24.3 million post-money valuation with 18% dilution, according to Carta's July 2026 benchmark. PitchBook-NVCA, measuring a different population, puts the median round size nearer $3.0 million on an $18.4 million pre-money.

Both are correct. They measure different things. This is the full picture, with the method attached to every number, so you can quote it in a meeting without being corrected.

<!-- truncate -->

## Key takeaways

- **Median seed: $4.1M raised, $24.3M post, 18% dilution** (Carta, July 2026).
- **Median pre-seed pre-money: $7.7M** (PitchBook-NVCA, Q3 2025).
- AI companies tend to price **well above** the median. In our reading, most non-AI B2B SaaS lands below it.
- Seed dilution moves far less than round size or valuation. **Round size moves; dilution barely does.**
- Never quote a median without its source. The two headline datasets differ by more than $1 million on round size.

## Why the two sources disagree

This trips up founders constantly, so it is worth being precise.

**PitchBook-NVCA** tracks priced equity rounds across the US venture market, sourced from filings, announcements and reporting. It skews toward rounds that were announced and priced.

**Carta** sees what runs through its cap-table software. That is a broader set of transactions including many that are never announced, and it covers a population that self-selects into using Carta.

Neither is wrong. They are different denominators. The practical rule: **quote the source with the number every time.** A founder who says "the median seed is $4.1 million" to an investor who reads PitchBook will spend the next two minutes on a definitional argument instead of on their company.

## Dilution is the stable number

Round sizes swing with the cycle. Valuations swing harder. Dilution barely moves.

Median seed dilution sits around 18% in Carta's July 2026 data, and in our experience it has stayed in a similar band across very different market conditions. That is not a coincidence; it is a norm. Investors need a meaningful position, founders need to survive to Series A with enough equity to stay motivated, and the negotiation resolves to roughly a fifth.

The useful implication: **dilution is the constraint, so solve for it.** Decide what you are willing to give up, then work backwards to the raise amount at a defensible valuation. Founders who solve for valuation first end up either raising too little at a good headline number, or taking excessive dilution to hit a round size they picked arbitrarily.

A worked example at the median:

| | |
|---|---|
| Target dilution | 18% |
| Post-money | $24.3M |
| Raise | $4.4M |
| Pre-money | $19.9M |

If you need $2.5M, the median valuation gives away about 10%, which most investors will resist as too small a position. If you need $6M, you are at 25% and should expect a hard conversation. The round size and the valuation have to be consistent with a normal dilution outcome, or one side pushes back.

## Sector spread beats the median

The single most misleading thing a founder can do with this data is benchmark against the market median.

AI companies tend to raise at **well above** median valuations. In our reading, most non-AI B2B SaaS lands below it. Deep tech, consumer, marketplaces and fintech all price differently, and geography adds another layer: in our experience a European seed often prices below a US seed at the same stage and traction.

Benchmark against your sector, your stage, your geography and your traction level. If you cannot find that specific comparison, the honest answer in a meeting is "here is the market median and here is why we think we sit above or below it", which is a far stronger position than quoting a number that does not apply to you.

## Pre-seed is the noisiest stage

The $7.7M median pre-seed pre-money hides enormous variance, for structural reasons:

- Most pre-seed happens on **SAFEs and convertible notes**, which have caps rather than valuations. A cap is not a valuation, and treating them as equivalent distorts every dataset.
- "Pre-seed" is not a defined term. It covers $150K friends-and-family and $2M institutional pre-seed, which are different asset classes.
- Much of it is never reported anywhere.

Practical guidance at pre-seed: anchor on the **cap and the dilution** you are accepting, not on an implied valuation. A $1M raise on an $8M post-money cap is 12.5% if it converts at the cap (about 11% on a pre-money cap), and you should plan around that number, not around the $8M.

## The number that should actually drive your planning

Not the valuation. The **gap to the next round**.

The median gap between primary rounds runs to roughly 774 days, about 2.1 years, in Carta's data. Roughly 15-20% of seed companies reach a priced Series A within 24 months.

That means the correct question is not "what is the market paying?" It is "how much do I need to reach the evidence threshold for the next round, with 24 months of runway and a margin for a process that takes six months?"

Solve that, then check the answer against the dilution norm. If the number you need implies 30% dilution, you have either a valuation problem or a plan that is too expensive - and it is better to find that out now than in month nine of your raise.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the average seed round in 2026?**
Carta's July 2026 benchmark puts the median at $4.1 million on a $24.3 million post-money. PitchBook-NVCA puts the median priced seed round nearer $3.0 million. Use the figure that matches your population.

**How much equity do you give up in a seed round?**
Median dilution is about 18%. Anything above 25% should prompt a hard look at either the raise amount or the valuation.

**What is a normal pre-seed valuation in 2026?**
Median pre-seed pre-money is around $7.7 million, but the variance is enormous and most pre-seed runs on SAFEs with caps rather than priced valuations.

**Do AI startups really raise at higher valuations?**
Yes, AI companies tend to price well above the median. The premium is real and concentrated.

**Should I raise more at a lower valuation or less at a higher one?**
Solve for dilution and runway first. You need 24 months and enough capital to clear the next round's evidence bar. Optimise the headline number only after those two are satisfied.
